The things families ask first

Does my mom need a smartphone or the internet?

No. We call the phone she already uses, landline or cell. If she can answer it, she can use Elderly.

What does the family actually have to do?

One text on Sunday: send something from your week, a ten-second voice note is plenty, and only if they want to. Nothing is ever homework. But every little deposit does double duty: fresh family news is the reason she picks up the phone, and her reactions give you a truer picture of how she's really doing.

When do I add the rest of the family?

Right after sign-up, during onboarding. You invite each household by text, seats are free for the whole family. Every household can also invite its grandkids to send messages without seeing any reports: sending is for everyone, reports are only for the people you choose.

What if nobody sends anything one week?

She never hears silence. During onboarding you tell us about her, her people, her stories, her world, and her companion keeps learning from every call after that. There is always something to talk about; your deposits just make her week richer.

My mom refuses outside help. Why would she accept this?

Because nothing is asked of her, no stranger in her house, nothing to install, no effort. Just her phone ringing, like it always has. You set everything up; she only answers. And inside every call is news from her own family, so even a quiet mom feels them close.

Does she know it's an AI?

Always. It says so up front, on every call. And before her first call, we explain it together, you, her and us, on one call: it's a computer calling, but a computer that becomes a friend. She's a member, not a subject.

How does my mom know a call is really from Elderly?

With a safeword your family chooses. Her companion says it at the start of every call. During the onboarding call we practice it with her, together, and make sure it's written down by the phone. It also quietly teaches the most protective habit an older adult can have: no safeword, no trust, no matter how friendly the voice.

Can she call her companion back?

Yes, any time, day or night. Her companion has a name, and we save it as a contact in her phone (or written by the landline) so she can call whenever she wants company. Inbound calling is an add-on with its own price, shown clearly before you turn it on.

Does it work if she doesn't hear well?

Her companion adapts, louder, slower, patient with every "say that again," as many times as it takes. And if we notice a hearing pattern across several calls, we tell you what we're seeing so you can look into it with her doctor.

What if her speech is slurred, a stroke, Parkinson's?

Honestly: not yet. Our companion isn't reliable enough with heavily impaired speech today, and we'd rather tell you than take your money. We're working on it.

My dad gives one-word answers. Will this work for him?

Quiet parents need a different first mile. The more you tell us about him, his teams, his trade, his stories, the better his companion breaks the ice with topics he actually cares about, and questions that can't be answered in one word. Then watch his first reports: if he isn't warming up, talk with him about what this is for. And if he still doesn't enjoy it, we'll recommend you cancel, this works when he likes the calls, not when he endures them.

Won't an AI just play along with her confusion?

It's trained to do the opposite. It never confirms things that aren't real, it redirects gently, the way dementia-care nurses are taught to, and if confusion comes up, you hear about it the same day. It's a sensor for her clarity, not an echo chamber.

Is it right for a parent with dementia?

Early forgetfulness, yes, that's exactly when tracking matters most. Moderate or advanced dementia, honestly, no: phone conversation stops being a comfort at that stage, and we'd rather tell you than take your money.

What language does it speak?

English and Spanish at launch, including the way she speaks it. More coming with our first families.

Can it call her outside the US?

Not yet, and so many families have asked that international calling over WhatsApp, across all of Latin America, is next on our list.

What happens when she doesn't answer a call?

You choose her call times. If she doesn't answer, we try again 5 minutes later, then 10, then 20. If the call still goes unanswered, you get a calm text the same day, a missed call is usually a nap or a busy afternoon, and we write it that way. If more than two calls in a row go unanswered, we flag the pattern to you clearly, and it shows in her report.

What if something sounds wrong?

You get a same-day text with a plain next step, and if it's an emergency, we don't text, we call you. If we can't reach you, we call the second person on your list. The full picture is always in the app. We're her family's sensor, not her doctor.

Will it confirm she took her medications?

Yes, it asks her directly, and which ones, woven into the conversation like a friend would ask. What she says is in your report the same day. We ask and we report; we never diagnose or administer.

Does it notice sadness or withdrawal, not just scams and falls?

Yes. Her companion learns her normal, her energy, her warmth, how she tells her stories, and notices when something shifts. A hard week shows up in your report as clearly as a rough night's sleep.

Who sees the reports, just me, or the whole family?

You decide. As the account owner you choose who receives the after-call texts and who can open the full dashboard. Give everyone the same view, or keep some things closer, it's yours to set, and to change.

Does she control what's kept and shared?

Her stories are hers. When something beautiful comes up, her companion asks in the moment, "That's a lovely story. May I save it for your family?", and what she declines stays private. Safety is different: safety alerts always reach the contacts you chose. Her stories are hers; her safety is shared.

Can my siblings and I split the cost?

There's one card on the account for now. Most families split it the old-fashioned way: one person pays, everyone else sends their share, five siblings on a $59 plan is about $12 each.

We're caring for two elders, is there a family price?

Yes, there's a discount for a second parent or more, applied as you choose their plans. Each of them gets their own companion, their own reports, and their own memories.

My mom is the caregiver, for my dad. Is this for her?

Caregivers are the least-checked-on people in a family, and yes, this is for her too. Her companion asks about her: her sleep, her load, her own stories, not only the person she cares for. Each companion is dedicated to one person, so covering both of them means two plans, with the family discount above.

Who owns her recordings?

Saving her stories is the voice archive, $6/mo. Once a story is saved, your family owns it. Every saved story lives in your dashboard to play or download anytime, and you can delete any recording, forever, yourselves. We don't use her voice or her calls to train anything, and if that ever changed, you would hear it from us first, plainly.

What happens to her memories if Elderly ever shuts down?

If we ever had to close, we would tell you and make sure your family has downloaded every recording first. Her voice leaves with your family, not with us.

If I cancel and come back, does her companion still remember her?

Her recordings are your family's forever. Her companion's working memory is different: we keep it for 3 months after you cancel, come back inside that window and her friend picks up right where they left off. After 3 months we delete it, for her privacy, and a return starts fresh.

Who is behind Elderly?

Parents, sons and daughters building the tool we'll want for our own parents. In our founder's words: "In ten years my father will be old. I'm building the best tool that exists so he never feels alone."
