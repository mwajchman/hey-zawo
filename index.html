      “Will my parent actually like talking to an AI?”

      Fair question, and here is the honest answer: they aren't really answering for the AI. They're answering for the family news inside it.

      They're in on it, always

      Before the first call you tell them yourself, with the words we hand you. On the first call the friend asks their permission to call and to record, out loud, and from then on it opens every single call by saying it's an AI and that the call is recorded. A member, never a subject.

      The first week is free

      A full week of the exact plan, on us. Cancel before day 7 and you pay nothing, and there are no contracts, ever. If they don't light up, you stop. You'll hear the answer in their voice.

      And it isn't for everyone

      Elderly is built for parents who are still themselves: sharp, funny, just alone too much. For someone living with moderate or advanced dementia, a friend on the phone isn't the right tool yet, and we'll tell you so.

          The things families ask first.

          Every question we've been asked, grouped so you can find yours.

          Does my parent need a smartphone or the internet?

          No. We call the phone they already use, landline or cell. If they can answer it, they can use Elderly.

          What does the family actually have to do?

          Nothing is required: anyone can add a bit of news for the next call in the app, and only if they want to. Nothing is ever homework. But every little deposit does double duty: fresh family news is the reason they pick up, and their reactions give you a truer picture of how they're really doing.

          When do I add the rest of the family?

          Right after sign-up, during onboarding. You invite each household by email, and the first six seats are included. If your family is bigger, each extra seat is $3 a month. Grandkids can send messages without seeing any reports: sending is for everyone, reports are only for the people you choose.

          What if nobody sends anything one week?

          They never hear silence. During onboarding you tell us about their people, their stories and their world, and the friend keeps learning from every call after that. There is always something to talk about; your deposits just make the week richer.

          My parent refuses outside help. Why would they accept this?

          Because nothing is asked of them: no stranger in the house, nothing to install, no effort. Just the phone ringing, like it always has. You set everything up; they only answer. And inside every call is news from their own family.

          What language does it speak?

          English and Spanish at launch, including the way they actually speak it. More coming with our first families.

          Can it call outside the US?

          Yes, in Mexico. The rest of Latin America is next. If your parent lives anywhere else, build the plan anyway and pick your country at the last step: nothing is created and nothing is charged, we keep the plan, and we write when we open there. There is also a waiting list on our Spanish site.

          Do they know it's an AI?

          Yes, twice over. Before the first call you tell them yourself, with the words we hand you. And then the friend says it out loud in its opening line on every single call, without being asked: its name, that it's an AI, and that the call is recorded for their family. It never pretends to be a person, and it never denies it if they ask again.

          Can they call their friend back?

          Yes, any time, day or night. The friend has a name, saved as a contact in their phone (or written by the landline) so they can call whenever they want company. Giving them their own incoming line is an extra we are still building. Write to hello@callelderly.com and we will put you on the list: when it opens we tell you what it costs, and nothing is added to your plan until you say yes.

          Does it work if they don't hear well?

          The friend adapts: louder, slower, patient with every "say that again," as many times as it takes. If we notice a hearing pattern across several calls, we tell you what we're seeing so you can look into it with their doctor.

          What if their speech is slurred, from a stroke or Parkinson's?

          Honestly: not yet. The friend isn't reliable enough with heavily impaired speech today, and we'd rather tell you than take your money. We're working on it.

          My parent gives one-word answers. Will this work?

          Quiet parents need a different first mile. The more you tell us about their teams, their trade and their stories, the better the friend breaks the ice with questions that can't be answered in one word. Watch the first reports: if they aren't warming up, we'll recommend you cancel. This works when they like the calls, not when they endure them.

          Won't an AI just play along with confusion?

          It's trained to do the opposite. It never confirms things that aren't real, it redirects gently, the way dementia-care nurses are taught to, and if confusion comes up, you hear about it the same day. It's a sensor for their clarity, not an echo chamber.

          Is it right for a parent with dementia?

          Early forgetfulness, yes: that's exactly when tracking matters most. Moderate or advanced dementia, honestly, no. Phone conversation stops being a comfort at that stage, and we'd rather tell you than take your money.

          How do they know a call is really from Elderly?

          With a safeword your family chooses. The friend says it at the start of every call. During the onboarding call we practice it together and make sure it's written down by the phone. It quietly teaches the most protective habit an older adult can have: no safeword, no trust, no matter how friendly the voice.

          What happens when they don't answer a call?

          You choose the call times. If there's no answer, we try again 5 minutes later, then 10, then 20. If the call still goes unanswered, you get a calm email the same day: a missed call is usually a nap or a busy afternoon, and we write it that way. More than two unanswered calls in a row and we flag the pattern clearly.

          What if something sounds wrong?

          You and everyone in the circle get an email right after the call, with a plain next step. The friend never waits in an emergency: it says to hang up and call emergency services. We're the family's sensor, not their doctor.

          Will it confirm they took their medications?

          Yes. It asks directly, and which ones, woven into the conversation like a friend would ask. The answer is in your report the same day. We ask and we report; we never diagnose or administer.

          Does it notice sadness or withdrawal, not just scams and falls?

          Yes. The friend learns their normal: energy, warmth, the way they tell their stories. It notices when something shifts. A hard week shows up in your report as clearly as a rough night's sleep.

          Who sees the reports: just me, or the whole family?

          You decide. As the account owner you choose who receives the after-call emails and who can open the full dashboard. Give everyone the same view, or keep some things closer. It's yours to set, and to change.

          Can my siblings and I split the cost?

          There's one card on the account for now. Most families split it the old-fashioned way: one person pays, everyone else sends their share. For example, five calls a week with 30-minute calls comes to $59 a month: between five siblings, about $12 each.

          One parent is the caregiver for the other. Is this for them too?

          Caregivers are the least-checked-on people in a family, so yes. The friend asks about them: their sleep, their load, their own stories, not only the person they care for. Each friend is dedicated to one person, so covering both means two plans.

          If I cancel and come back, does the friend still remember?

          Canceling the plan doesn't erase anything: while the account stays open, recordings are there to download and the friend picks up right where they left off. Closing the account is different, so download what you want to keep first.

          Do they control what's kept and shared?

          Their stories are theirs. When something beautiful comes up, the friend asks in the moment, "That's a lovely story. May I save it for your family?", and what they decline stays private. Safety is different: safety alerts always reach the contacts you chose.

          Is every call recorded, and who owns the recordings?

          Every call is recorded and transcribed. That is what the friend says out loud at the start of every call, and what it asks permission for on the first one. The recordings belong to your family: from the portal you can play or download any call while the account is open. What you don't get is the word-for-word transcript. You get the note and the signals, because that was the deal we made with them. The voice archive, $6 a month, is a different thing: it keeps the life stories she chooses to save in her own voice, so they survive the call they came from. We don't use their voice or their calls to train anything, and if that ever changed, you would hear it from us first, plainly.

          Can anyone at Elderly listen to the calls?

          Not while they are happening. Nobody at Elderly, and nobody in your family, listens in live. Afterwards is different, and we would rather say it than let you assume otherwise: a small number of people who run the service can open a recording or a transcript, and they do it for three reasons only, which are checking that someone really gave permission, working out why a call went wrong, and answering a support request from you. Every single time, the system writes down who opened it and when. Our vendors see what they need to do their job, which is set out in the Privacy Policy.

          What happens to the memories if Elderly ever shuts down?

          Here is the honest version. The written stories, the notes and the summaries are ours to hand over, and our Terms now commit us to giving you at least 30 days' notice and keeping the downloads working through it. The audio is the part we can't promise forever: the recordings sit with the company that places the calls, not on a shelf of ours. So the rule that actually protects you is the boring one. Download what matters to you while the account is open, and don't wait for us to be closing.

          Who is behind Elderly?

          Parents, sons and daughters building the tool we'll want for our own parents. In our founder's words: "In ten years my father will be old. I'm building the best tool that exists so he never feels alone."
